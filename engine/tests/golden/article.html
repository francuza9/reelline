Rover finds rock layers that formed in an ancient lake

NASA's Perseverance rover has found 20 distinct rock layers at the edge of Jezero crater on Mars, the agency said in a news release. The layers are about 3 meters thick in total and are stacked like the pages of a book.

Scientists say the layers look like sediment that settled slowly in a lake, which means water stood in the crater for a long time. The rover used its camera and a small ground-penetrating radar to measure each layer, and it has already sent the first images to Earth.

The team plans to collect a sample from the thickest layer later this year. NASA says the sample will be sealed in a titanium tube and left on the surface so that a future mission can bring it back to laboratories on Earth.

Perseverance landed in Jezero crater in February 2021. Since then it has driven more than 40 kilometers and collected 30 rock and soil samples. Mission scientists say that the new layers are among the clearest records of the ancient lake that the rover has seen so far, and that they will study the images for several months before deciding where to drill next.

The mission is run by NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory in California. Images and data from the rover are public and can be viewed on the mission website.
